EGR 105 · Semester Project 2025–26
Software · Hardware · Robotics

Robot Artist

An end-to-end system that converts any digital image into physical robot movements on paper. The pipeline: Canny Edge Detection turns the image grayscale and extracts every edge as an XY coordinate → a friendly-neighbor clustering algorithm groups nearby points and orders them by proximity to minimize total travel distance → a Python Turtle simulation lets you watch the robot "draw" on screen before touching hardware → the final optimized coordinate list is uploaded to an ESP32 CNC machine over WiFi, which moves a marker across paper to recreate the image.

My specific contributions: built robot.py — the Turtle simulation engine that executes paths step-by-step, lifts the pen between disconnected segments, and renders the drawing in real time — and robot_safety.py — the Tkinter control panel with directional movement, pen up/down, emergency stop, reset, set home, and go-home buttons. Also led the hardware debugging that bridged the software pipeline with the physical ESP32 system. Tested on the Duke logo and a cloud logo, ~20 runs each, iterating on coordinate scaling and pen-down threshold to eliminate gaps in the drawing.

EGR 121 · Design Challenge 2 Spring 2026
Mechanical Design · Fabrication · Team

Team SEED — Sunflower De-Sheller

A human-powered mechanical device that cracks and separates sunflower seeds, scored on clean kernels per minute over a 3-minute performance window. The team began with sticky-note brainstorming, built a morphological matrix of cracking and separating mechanisms, and ran a Pugh Screening Matrix to compare a gear-driven crusher, an inclined rolling mechanism, an abrasive drum, and a compression press. The press won.

Final machine: a spring-loaded metal crushing plate descends via a wooden crushing spool onto a grooved seed holder that locks seeds on their side — the orientation that maximizes shell crack with minimum kernel damage. Cracked seeds fall into a mesh separating bin where small kernels fall through the grid while larger shell fragments stay on top. A movement tray with a stub guide slides the seed holder into position repeatably. The entire machine fits inside a standard Foundry storage bin. Iterated through multiple prototypes after discovering early designs were too complex for the available time.

EGR 121 · Design Challenge 4 Spring 2026
Electromechanical · Arduino · Green Design

SportBot — Sensor-Activated Rube Goldberg

One subteam's contribution to a larger group Rube Goldberg chain — five teams' SportBots performed in sequence, each receiving a ball, triggering two actions, and passing it on. The overall theme was a sports relay; our machine was a wood-and-cardboard structure built to 40–80 cm elevation using cylindrical wooden posts, decorated with repurposed 3D-print supports painted as seaweed.

Action 1 — Switch → LED: The incoming ball rolls down a ramp and hits a mechanical switch/lever. This completes a simple circuit that turns on LED lights mounted on the structure — a visual signal that the machine has been activated.
Action 2 — Ultrasonic Sensor → Elevator: As the ball continues rolling, it passes through the detection zone of an ultrasonic distance sensor (HC-SR04). The Arduino UNO reads the echo signal, detects the ball's presence, waits a minor delay, then drives a DC motor via an L293D motor driver to raise an elevator platform — lifting the ball upward and delivering it to the next team's SportBot in the chain.

The circuit — designed in Tinkercad — runs on a 9V battery and uses the Arduino for the sensor-triggered elevator (the digital action) while the LED switch circuit operates as a standalone analog loop. Zero new plastic used: structure from scrap wood, cardboard, and reclaimed print supports.

EGR 121 · Design Challenge 3 Spring 2026
SolidWorks · DFM · Manufacturing

Happy Meals — Injection-Molded Toy

A fully engineered McDonald's Happy Meal toy — from concept to 3D-printed prototype — designed to real production constraints. The toy is a rocket ship with a detachable base, conceived to appeal to ages 3+ with a space theme. Every separable part had to pass a choke hazard test device (1.25" internal diameter cylinder with slanted inner plane): if the part fits entirely inside, it's a choking hazard; it must protrude.

Technical deliverables: individual SolidWorks CAD parts for each team member, three-view engineering drawings with Critical-to-Fit dimensions called out, worst-case tolerance stack-up analysis on all mating features, DFM review ensuring wall thickness, draft angles, and parting lines are injection-mold compatible, and a full manufacturing plan with per-part material cost, machine hours, and production volume estimates. Presented and demoed at the live Duke Toy Fair tradeshow booth with a one-page marketing sheet.

SolidWorks Injection Molding DFM Tolerance Analysis 3D Printing Manufacturing Plan Tradeshow Pitch

EGR 121 · Design Challenge 1 Spring 2026
Reverse Engineering · Systems Analysis

Machine Anatomy — RC Car Teardown

A full mechanical and electrical teardown of a Blade Amphibious RC car — a water-and-land-capable vehicle with a sealed chassis, multi-stage gear drivetrain, wireless controller, and LED wheel lighting. The car and controller were disassembled in sequence to maintain part traceability, with every component catalogued into functional systems: body, drivetrain, wheels, electronics, and control.

Deliverables: a 28-part costed Bill of Materials (manufacturing cost estimated at $9.29–$10.84 against a $30 retail price — ~35% of retail, consistent with the 20–35% rule of thumb), a hierarchical Structured Assembly diagram breaking the product into assembly → subassembly → part levels, and hand-sketched exploded views of the controller, chassis body, drivetrain compartment, and wheel subassembly. Key engineering takeaways: the multi-stage gear reduction efficiently transfers motor torque to all four wheels; standardized fasteners throughout keep assembly cost low; and the sealed chassis achieves waterproofing without a separate gasket system.
